---

## 1. Summary of the manuscript

The paper proposes a "Will-Matter" substance dualism in which every entity is a combination of two entangled essences, Will (immaterial) and Matter (material). Each essence can be intrinsic or extrinsic and the Will can be active or passive, which yields four "states of existence": Active Independent, Passive Independent, Active Dependent and Passive Dependent. The author then maps these four states onto the REA (Resource-Event-Agent) ontology of McCarthy: Agent = Active Independent, Resource = Passive Independent, Event = a composite of Active Dependent ("Institutional Agent") and Passive Dependent ("Institutional Resource"). The resulting "DREAM" meta-model is claimed to lift REA from a domain ontology for economic exchange to a foundational ontology able to describe any "Context". A dinner-table example illustrates the model, and a discussion compares the DREAM constructs with the REA constructs (including the classification of Agent, Resource and Event as rigid sortals in UFO terms, and the reclassification of service and rights as Events rather than Resources).

The underlying idea, giving REA an explicit philosophical foundation and reading its three primitives as modes of existence rather than as economic roles, is original and potentially interesting to the REA, value-modeling and ontology-engineering communities. The execution, however, falls well short of the standard needed for publication, on every axis reviewed below.

**Verdict.** The manuscript should not be submitted in this form. The blocking problems, in order of severity, are: (1) unattributed verbatim text from identifiable sources (a publication-ethics issue, see 6.4); (2) the absence of any formalization or evaluation of the proposed ontology; (3) technical errors in the ontological analysis (UFO categories, cardinalities, contradictions between sections); (4) the quality of the English, which in many places prevents the reader from understanding the claims. A thorough rewrite by the author, followed by professional language editing, is needed.

## 3. Writing and grammar

### 3.1 General assessment

The English is not at publication level. The problems are systematic rather than occasional; a grammatical error appears in most paragraphs and several sentences have no finite verb at all. A native-speaker or professional edit is required before any submission, but editing alone will not suffice, because many sentences are semantically unclear as well as ungrammatical (for example the definition of Active Will on p. 17: "the directive-distributive link of a specific physical process to the occurrence of a specific phenomenal experience that might be rendered or projected in a specific physical process that activates another cycle of a specific phenomenal experience").

### 3.3 Word choice, register and tone

- "represent" is used throughout where "present" is meant (abstract, pp. 4, 8, 11, 14, 16, 19, 20, 27). Similarly "reify"/"reification" is used for "reinterpret", "assimilate ... by" for "integrate ... with", and "mode" in the title where "model" appears to be meant. These are lexical errors that change the meaning.
- The register is often informal or conversational for a scientific paper: "Maybe after years of reluctance" (p. 3), "less than one page" (p. 15), "Here you can comprehend the meaning ... as what your common sense suggests" (p. 15), "Let us describe the meaning of becoming a little bit more" (p. 16), "Right here we should clarify" (p. 24), "Now you might be able to see the DREAM in the following sentence" (p. 28).
- The introduction contains a polemical, unsupported paragraph blaming "materialist thinking domination" for pollution, climate anomalies, migration, fundamentalism, racism and populism (p. 3). This is not argued, not cited, and will alienate reviewers of any venue. It should be removed.
- The paper alternates between "we" (body) and "I" (acknowledgement); with a single author, choose one and keep it.
- Capitalization of the paper's own terms is inconsistent (Context/context, Will/will, Matter/matter, Event/event, Ontology/ontology, meta-model/metamodel). Define a convention and apply it.
- Gendered "his labour" (p. 12) should be "their labour" or the example rewritten.

## 4. Structure and presentation

### 4.1 Organisation

- A **table of contents** (p. 2) is not appropriate in a journal or conference manuscript; remove it. In the TOC the top-level headings are unnumbered while sub-headings are numbered; in the body the top-level headings are numbered. Make them consistent.
- The **Acknowledgement** sits on the title page between the keywords and the introduction. It belongs after the conclusion.
- The preamble of Section 2 says the section "summarizes two elements of background information" but the section has four parts (dualism, ontology and IS, REA, Context). The opening of p. 8 refers to "the second part of the introduction" although it is in Section 2.
- **Section 2.2.1 (HDMI model, Figure 1) and the pattern hierarchy (Figure 3)** are a five-page digression drawn from the author's own earlier workshop paper. They are only used again in Figure 9, and nothing in the DREAM ontology depends on them. Either cut them to one paragraph and one figure, or justify them explicitly.
- **Section 2.1 (dualism survey, pp. 4-7)** is a one-sided catalogue of pro-dualist chapters, most from a single edited volume. It never states what the paper needs from dualism, and it surveys mind-body and person-body dualism, whereas Section 3 attributes Will to wood and nails, which is closer to Schopenhauerian voluntarism or panpsychism than to any dualism surveyed. The background therefore does not support the theory that follows.
- **Redundancy.** The Section 3 opening (p. 14) repeats the road-map sentence from p. 4 verbatim; the first paragraph of 3.1 repeats the abstract verbatim; the "Context of sociology" example appears in full on p. 16 and again on p. 28; the definitions of Agent/Resource/Event in 4.2-4.4 are repeated again in 5.1-5.3 with the same wording. Roughly a quarter of the text can be removed without loss.
- **Missing sections.** There is no Related Work section on prior attempts to give REA an ontological foundation (Geerts & McCarthy 2002; Gailly, Laurier & Poels 2008; Hruby 2006; the UFO-based analyses), no Evaluation section, and no Limitations/Future Work in the conclusion.
- The "Discussion" (Section 5) is in fact a construct-by-construct comparison with REA and should be titled as such; a real discussion of implications, threats and limitations is absent.

## 5. Content

### 5.1 Contribution and novelty

The positioning is promising: REA's three primitives are treated by REA2 (Laurier et al. 2018) as roles; this paper asks whether they can instead be seen as modes of existence that any context requires, which would make REA usable beyond economic exchange. That question is worth asking. But the paper does not deliver on it: the Will-Matter ontology is asserted rather than argued, the mapping to REA is asserted rather than derived, and no evaluation shows that DREAM can still model what REA models (an exchange) or that it can model something REA cannot.

### 5.2 The philosophical foundation (Sections 2.1 and 3.4)

1. **The argument for the existence of Will (p. 18) is circular.** Step 1 is "Existence of Will is True" and step 5, the conclusion, is the same proposition. Steps 2-4 assume that judging requires a Will, which is what is to be shown. As it stands this is not an argument; it must either be removed or replaced by a genuine argument (for example, one of the arguments the author surveys in 2.1).
2. **The paper's dualism is not the dualism it surveys.** Sections 2.1.3-2.1.4 concern mind-body and person-body dualism. Section 3 attributes a (passive) Will to every material object, including nails and energy (pp. 18, 24). That position is Schopenhauer's voluntarism or a form of panpsychism, and the empirical work cited (Buettner's ECG study, psychiatric dualism) gives it no support. The author needs to state precisely which thesis is adopted and cite literature for that thesis.
3. **"Intentionality" is misused.** In philosophy of mind, intentionality means "aboutness" (directedness of mental states at objects), not intention or desire. The Will Matrix (3.4.1) defines intentionality as "the desire or want of the entity" and cites Lund (2014) for it; Lund does not define it that way. The matrix should use "intention" or "volition", and the citation must be checked.
4. **Historical claims are inaccurate.** Nietzsche is listed as a follower of Platonic/Cartesian dualism (p. 5); Nietzsche is among the most prominent critics of exactly that dualism. Schopenhauer's Will/Representation is normally read as a double-aspect view, not substance dualism. Schaffer (2010), cited for a "monistic-materialistic phenomenology of Energy" (p. 17), is about priority monism (the whole is prior to its parts) and says nothing about energy or materialism. The claim that the free-will debate in Western philosophy originates with the scholastics (p. 17) ignores the Stoics and Augustine. The list of "contemporary philosophers" of the will (Moore, Schlick, Campbell, ...) is taken from a 1981 psychiatry literature review and is not contemporary.
5. **Contradictions.** Dualism is said to be "in opposition to" mentalism and, in the same sentence, to "entail" mentalism (p. 4). Neuroscience is said to find dualism unpopular and, in the same sentence, to have dualism "dominant in most of their work" (p. 6). "Dual-aspectism" is presented as a version of dualism and defined as "a monist reality" (p. 7).
6. The argument that the wide historical distribution of dualism shows its "authenticity" (p. 5) is an appeal to popularity, not evidence.

### 5.3 The Will-Matter ontology (Section 3)

1. **Definitions are circular or absent.** "Entity is defined as a unique combination of existence of its Will and Matter" (3.2) defines an entity in terms of its own Will and Matter. "A glass of water is an independent Entity as it has the Matter of a glass of water intrinsically" (3.3). "Independent" is defined by "intrinsic Matter", and "intrinsic" is never defined except as what independent entities have.
2. **Passive Will for inanimate objects** is introduced by analogy with inertia and reactivity (p. 24), with the description reversed ("they resist keeping that state" should presumably be "they tend to keep that state"). If Will is to be attributed to all matter, the paper must say what work the concept does that "disposition" or "property" does not, and must explain why this is still a dualism rather than a property dualism.
3. **The four-state requirement for a Context** ("any Context needs at least one entity from each state of existence to exist", p. 16) is stipulated without justification and then used as the key premise for mapping REA. It also seems to make every Context require a human (see 5.5, item 3).
4. **Idealist implication.** "The Event that no one is aware of that by any means (even imagination) never occurred" (p. 24) contradicts the realist tone elsewhere and the conclusion's claim that geological Contexts "without any Agent(s) involved" can be handled by adding an observer.
5. "Consciousness is perceived as the state that the entity is aware that is aware of its awareness" (p. 18) is unintelligible; state the definition clearly and cite it correctly.

### 5.4 The DREAM ontology (Section 4)

1. **Kind of ontology.** The paper calls DREAM, in different places, a "foundational ontology" (abstract, conclusion), an "upper-level ontology" (p. 14), a "higher-level ontology" (pp. 3, 19), and a "core ontology" (Figure 9). These are different things in the literature (Guarino 1998; Guizzardi 2005). A four-category model that presupposes UFO for its relations (p. 22) is not itself a foundational ontology. Decide, define, and cite.
2. **UFO classifications are incorrect (Sections 5.1-5.3).** The paper states that Agent, Resource and Event are each "a RigidSortal category and considered as a Kind based on the UFO". In UFO a Kind is a rigid substantial sortal that supplies a principle of identity for endurants. (a) An Event is a perdurant; UFO-B gives events their own category, and an event is never a Kind. (b) "Resource" cannot be a Kind because it classifies things of different kinds (wood, nails, energy, a table) and supplies no principle of identity; this is exactly why REA2 treats Economic Resource as a role. The paper's own dinner-table example shows anti-rigid behaviour: the table is first an Institutional Resource and then a Resource, i.e. the same individual changes classification, which is impossible for a Kind. (c) "Agent = Human" would make Agent a Kind only if Agent is identical to Person, in which case the construct adds nothing over Person. The section asserts the difference with Laurier et al. (2018) but never refutes their argument.
3. **The term "Institutional"** for the dependent parts of an Event is a poor choice. In social ontology (Searle) and in the REA literature itself "institutional" refers to socially constituted facts, commitments and agreements. Here it means only "existentially dependent". "Dependent Agent part" / "Dependent Resource part", or "Experience" / "Product", would avoid confusion.
4. **Cardinalities.** Text says every Event must connect to at least one other Event; Figure 7 shows 0..* on both ends of "Continuity". Text says Agent-Event is "zero to many"; the figure shows 1..* at the Agent end and 0..* at the Event end, which is fine, but then the text should say so. The Resource "Linkage" and Agent "Responsibility" self-associations are shown but never explained.
5. **Duality is dropped without discussion.** REA's defining axiom is duality (every event is requited by another). DREAM replaces it by "continuity" and one sentence (p. 21) says duality "is subject to continuity of Events". Since the paper claims REA remains an instantiation of DREAM, it must show how duality, stock-flow and participation are recovered, ideally as constraints on the DREAM meta-model.
6. **Reading the meta-model.** "The result of act of Agent on Resource is Event" (p. 21) is a causal claim inserted into a structural model; the arrows in Figure 7 are said to show chronology, which UML associations do not express. Either use a process notation for the temporal part or remove the temporal reading.

### 5.5 Comparison with REA (Section 5)

1. **Rights and services are removed from Resource** (5.2, 5.3.1). In REA and in ISO/IEC 15944-4 the economic event is the transfer of rights (ownership, custody) in a resource. Once rights are not resources, the paper owes the reader a DREAM model of an ordinary sale: who transfers what, and what the duality is. No such example is given; the only example (building and using a table) involves a single agent and no exchange at all. As it stands the paper has not shown that DREAM covers REA's original scope, which undermines the "upgrade" claim.
2. **Service as an Event** may be defensible (and is compatible with Service-Dominant Logic, though the foundational Vargo & Lusch 2004 paper is not cited), but "service is equivalence with the Economic Event" (p. 25) is stronger than SDL says and is asserted, not argued.
3. **Agent = human only** (p. 23) contradicts ISO/IEC 15944-4, where Person includes organizations (legal persons), contradicts REA practice (companies as agents), contradicts the paper's own 5.3.1 ("an individual or an organization"), and rules out software agents, which matters for the AI motivation given in the introduction. The exclusion is derived from a single ISO glossary note ("Automatons are excluded") and needs a real argument.
4. **The Will of the same Agent changes per Event, but the Agent is a rigid Kind** (5.4, p. 26). The paper needs a notion of state or disposition (UFO has "intrinsic moments" for exactly this) instead of repeating the Agent class three times in Figure 8.
5. The comparison with REA2's independent/dependent views (Laurier et al.) is superficial; a table mapping REA, REA2 and DREAM constructs and axioms would help far more than the prose in 5.1-5.3.

### 5.6 Evaluation and validation

- There is **no formalization** (no axioms, no OWL/UML profile, no OntoUML model), although the meta-model is presented in a UML-like notation. The paper says (p. 19) that the ontology is "implementable to any other Context" but implements nothing.
- There is **no evaluation method**: no competency questions, no OntoClean/UFO-based analysis of the constructs, no case study beyond a toy, no comparison with modelling the same case in REA or REA2, no expert feedback.
- "We demonstrate the validity of the REA ontology in Contexts of exchange other than economic" (p. 14) is not supported by anything in the paper.
- For a journal such as Applied Ontology, Data & Knowledge Engineering, Information Systems Journal or the Journal of Information Systems, this gap alone would lead to rejection.

### 5.7 Overclaims to remove or qualify

"disastrous results" of materialism (p. 3); "full warehouses of solutions" (p. 3); "authenticity of this system of thinking" (p. 5); "we demonstrate the validity" (p. 14); "an Ontology that has been verified" (p. 20); "we upgraded the REA ontology into a foundational ontology that is able to represent any Context" (p. 27).

## 6. References and attribution

### 6.1 Reference list quality

The list is not in any consistent style. Problems that occur throughout: stray double full stops (". ."); some titles in quotation marks and others not; missing volume/issue numbers for most journal articles; missing editors and "In:" for all chapters of Lavazza & Robinson (2014); missing publishers or page ranges; "et al." used inside the reference list (Nelson et al. 2012; Moody et al. 2003), which is never acceptable; non-sources given as sources ("philpapers", "philosophersimprint", "independent"); ISO/IEC entry has no title or standard number; a stray opening parenthesis in the Taliaferro entry; author names in capitals (WALKER); mangled initials ("A.e Solvberg").

### 6.2 In-text citation style

Citations reproduce full author lists with initials, e.g. "(Laurier, W., Kiehn, J., and Polovina, S., 2018)", "(McDaniel M. and V. C. Storey, 2019)", "(Lindland O. I., G. Sindre, and A.e Solvberg, 1994)", "(Lowe E. J., 2009)". Use the author-year form of the target venue ("Laurier et al., 2018"). Several citations sit after the sentence's full stop, or the full stop is missing before them. "Uwe Meixner in (Meixner, 2004)" style constructions should be "Meixner (2004)".

## 7. Other aspects

- **Venue fit and length.** At 31 pages with a five-page philosophy survey and a five-page conceptual-modelling digression, the manuscript reads as a thesis chapter. A journal version should be 15-20 pages and focus on Sections 3-5 plus an evaluation. A workshop version (e.g. VMBO, where the author's earlier work appeared) could be 8-10 pages and present the Will-Matter/REA mapping as a position paper.
- **Two papers in one.** The philosophical proposal (Will-Matter) and the ontology-engineering proposal (DREAM) have different audiences and different standards of evidence. Consider splitting: a position paper on the Will-Matter ontology for a philosophy-of-information-systems audience, and a formalized, evaluated DREAM paper for the ontology/REA community.
- **Reproducibility / artefacts.** No formal model is provided. If an OWL/OntoUML version of DREAM exists, publish it (e.g. in the repository) and reference it; the repository this review is stored in currently contains OWL/TTL files for a different ontology only.
- **Anonymity.** If the target venue is double-blind, the acknowledgement, the self-citation "(Ramezani Besheli, 2018)" and the supervisor's name must be removed for review.
- **Abstract.** The abstract restates itself ("a foundational ontology that can be considered as a basic model that can be used to..."), contains an agreement error, and does not state a research question, method or result. Rewrite in the form: problem, gap, proposal, method, result, implication.
- **Keywords.** "Information Science" is too broad; add "REA ontology", "foundational ontology", "conceptual modeling", "context".
- **Title.** "Mode" appears to be a typo for "Model"; the semicolon should be a colon; "based on the Will-Matter dualism" reads better as "grounded in Will-Matter dualism".

## 8. Conclusion and suggestions

**Conclusion.** The manuscript contains an original idea that is worth developing, but in its present form it is not ready for publication anywhere. The English requires a complete edit, the philosophical argument is circular in its key step and inconsistent with the literature it surveys, the ontological analysis misapplies UFO categories and drops REA's central axiom without demonstrating that exchange can still be modelled, there is no formalization or evaluation, and there are verified instances of unattributed verbatim text. A journal would reject it; a workshop would at best return it for major revision.

**Suggested actions, in priority order.**

1. **Fix attribution.** Quote or rewrite every passage listed in 6.4 and cite the actual sources (SEP entries, the NDPR review, Olivé, both Stapp texts, ISO 15944-4, the real source of the second REA paragraph).
2. **Replace the circular argument for Will (p. 18)** with either a genuine argument or an explicit statement that Will-Matter dualism is adopted as a working assumption, and make Section 2.1 support the actual thesis (Will in all matter), including opposing views.
3. **Decide what DREAM is** (core ontology committed to UFO, or foundational ontology) and use one term throughout; align Figure 9 with the text.
4. **Correct the UFO analysis:** Event is a perdurant, not a Kind; Resource (and probably Agent) are roles/mixins; model the changing Will of an Agent with moments/dispositions rather than repeated classes. Engage with Laurier et al.'s reasons for role-based classification instead of contradicting them by assertion.
5. **Show that DREAM still covers REA:** model a two-party sale (with transfer of rights) in DREAM, and show how duality, stock-flow and participation are recovered as constraints. Then add a non-economic example that REA cannot model, to support the generality claim.
6. **Add formalization and evaluation:** an OntoUML/OWL model, competency questions, and at least one comparative case study (REA vs REA2 vs DREAM), or expert evaluation.
7. **Resolve internal contradictions:** Agent = human vs organizations as agents; unobserved events never occurred vs geological contexts; continuity "at least one" vs 0..* in Figure 7; "opposition to mentalism" vs "entails mentalism".
8. **Restructure and shorten:** remove the table of contents and the polemic paragraph; compress 2.1 to about one page and 2.2.1 to one paragraph; remove the repeated passages; add Related Work, Evaluation and Limitations sections; move the acknowledgement to the end.
9. **Fix all figures and tables:** supply the missing Figure 2 or renumber; repair the empty cross-reference on p. 20; caption Table 1 properly and add the first column header; regenerate Figures 1, 3, 4, 9 as vector graphics; correct typos inside Figures 7, 8 and 9.
